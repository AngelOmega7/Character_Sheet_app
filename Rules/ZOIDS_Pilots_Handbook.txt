==============================================================================
                         ZOIDS: PILOT'S HANDBOOK
                         Current Campaign Rules
==============================================================================

Steel claws strike the earth. A particle cannon gathers light between armored
jaws. Within the cockpit, a pilot must decide whether to fire, raise a shield,
or save enough Energy to survive the next exchange.

Your Zoid is more than a weapon. It is a fighting machine with its own
strengths, a chassis you can improve, and the center of a growing collection
of equipment, forms, and hard-earned experience. This book explains how to
build your Pilot and manage your Zoid for use in the ZOIDS Roleplaying Game.

==============================================================================
CHAPTER 1: BECOMING A PILOT
==============================================================================

A pilot brings judgment, training, and personality to a Zoid's mechanical
strength. Begin with a name, a pilot style, and the details that distinguish
your character from every other warrior in the cockpit.

STARTING RESOURCES
------------------
A new pilot begins with 10,000g and may choose one free starting Zoid:

  Command Wolf       Redler             Gun Sniper
  Rev Raptor         Shield Liger       Zaber Fang Old

Your starter costs no gold, Core, or Parts. Its Zi Data is added to your known
designs. You begin without an Organoid unless the GM grants one.

NOTE: Your GM may, at their discretion, authorize or require a particular
starting Zoid, even if it is not included in the above list.

PILOT STYLE AND LEVEL
---------------------
Choose Fighter, Tank, Scout, or Almighty. Your style's advancement table
determines the percentage bonuses you receive to Health, Armor, Reaction,
Range, and weapon accuracy. The Character Sheet calculates these values.

Pilot level ranges from 1 to 100. The NPC style supplies no style-progression
bonuses and is available for GM-created characters.

Your actual pilot level increases maximum Energy. A Type ability can increase
your effective level for style progression, but those bonus levels do not
increase maximum Energy. Use the available progression-table entries; no
advancement beyond the supplied tables is defined here.

PILOT ATTRIBUTES
----------------
At level 1, assign the scores 1, 2, and 3 among Physical (PHY), Mental (MEN),
and Social (SOC), using each score once. These attributes chiefly describe the
pilot's own capabilities rather than Zoid statistics.

For an attribute check, roll a d20 and add the relevant attribute. The result
is then compared by the GM to a hidden difficulty value in order to determine
success or failure.

For advantage, roll two d20s and use the higher result. For disadvantage, roll
two d20s and use the lower result. You can only benefit from one source of
advantage at a time, and likewise for disadvantage. Multiple sources granting
both advantage and disadvantage cancel each other out.

Mental adds directly to your Zoid's final Reaction stat (after other bonuses
are applied). While an Organoid is active, Mental also adds to Energy Per Turn.

Physical helps you withstand impacts and high-speed maneuvers. When your Zoid
reaches zero Health, roll d20 + PHY against 15 to avoid losing consciousness.
If unconscious, you may repeat this check each round to awaken. Physical also
governs the G-force checks for sustained speed boosts described in Chapter 3.

Mental helps with emergency System Down repairs and clearing weapon jams, in
addition to its Reaction and Organoid benefits. Social is used for roleplaying
interactions.

PERSONALITY AND STYLE CHANGES
-----------------------------
Your personality can guide the color of an Organoid or Wild Organoid System.
White is associated with joy, kindness, and nurturing; Red with aggression,
protectiveness, and firmness; Blue with energy, recklessness, and humor; Black
with reserve, strategy, pride, or sinister intent. These are roleplaying cues,
not compulsory behavior. The GM judges changes in an Organoid's color.

You may change pilot style at a Zoid Institute or another facility capable of
Zoid Development.

Record your personality, possessions, cargo, and campaign discoveries on your
pilot sheet. Gold, learned abilities, feats, and Organoid settings belong to
the pilot; individual Zoids retain their own combat resources.

ABILITIES AND FEATS
-------------------
You may have up to six active pilot abilities. You may own additional inactive
abilities, which provide no active bonuses. The sheet also records up to seven
feats. The advancement schedule below governs when you gain them.

ADVANCEMENT
-----------
The ZOIDS Roleplaying Game uses milestone advancement rather than experience
points. As a guideline, gain one level for every three notable battles won or
every ruin explored. The GM may also award a level for a significant narrative
accomplishment and may adjust the pace to suit the campaign. Maximum pilot
level is 100.

Begin with one Zoid ability at level 1. Gain a new ability at each subsequent
level, or use that gain to raise an owned ability by one rank. Higher ranks
require the following actual pilot levels:

  Ability rank      1      2      3      4      5
  Pilot level       1      5     10     15     20

Be sure to increase your style bonuses according to its progression table
every time you level up. You gain one Pilot Attribute point at levels 4, 8,
12, and every further multiple of 4. You gain a feat at level 10 and every ten
levels thereafter, if unlearned feats are available.

You may swap active abilities after completing a long rest. When you learn a
new ability, you may immediately swap it into an active slot. Learning a higher
rank replaces its lower rank as described in Chapter 6.


==============================================================================
CHAPTER 2: UNDERSTANDING YOUR ZOID
==============================================================================

A Zoid's catalog entry provides its starting statistics. Pilot training,
abilities, equipment, upgrades, and Organoids modify those values.

HEALTH
  Records the Zoid's remaining structural endurance. Maximum Health and current
  Health are separate values. Increasing a maximum does not normally repair
  existing damage. At zero Health, a player Zoid suffers System Down; see
  Chapter 4.

ARMOR
  Armor is the target number for most attacks. A modified attack roll equal
  to or greater than Armor hits. Armor and Defense are distinct; an effect
  that changes one does not change the other. Armor Breaker specifically lowers
  Armor, not Defense.

DEFENSE
  Subtract applicable Defense from the damage of a normal hit to Health.
  Defense cannot reduce that damage below 1. Critical hits normally bypass
  Defense; True Tank and other explicit exceptions can change this.
  Equipment that restricts its protection to particular damage types only
  contributes against those types. Some sources may abbreviate Melee, Laser,
  Explosive, Particle, and Bullet as M, L, E, P, and B for such equipment.

REACTION
  Records the Zoid's Reaction statistic. Some attacks explicitly target
  Reaction; Evasion Master penalizes attacks made against this statistic.
  Reaction also determines initiative and is added to area-attack avoidance
  rolls. A Pilot's Mental attribute may be added to a Zoid's Reaction.

SPEED AND RANGE
  Speed measures the Zoid's movement capability. Range establishes the range
  bands used by ranged-attacks. Specific weapon text may impose its own
  limits or targeting requirements. In combat, move up to Speed in meters per
  move action. Narrative travel uses Speed in kilometers per hour. See Chapter
  11 for terrain and special movement, and Chapter 4 for attack-range penalties.

ENERGY AND EPT
  Energy powers attacks and active equipment. Energy Per Turn, abbreviated EPT,
  is the amount restored at the beginning of a turn before upkeep is paid.

SHIELDS
  Shields provide a separate pool of protection while their sources are active.
  Shield damage is recorded separately from Health damage. You do not normally
  subtract Defense from damage done to shields.

SIZE
----
The size categories, from smallest to largest, are:

  S: Small   M: Medium   L: Large   LL: Large Large   XL: Extra Large

Size requirements are exact unless an effect names several categories. An
ability for Large Zoids does not automatically apply to LL or XL Zoids.

CARRY CAPACITY
--------------
Add the weight of all equipped removable items, whether active or inactive.
Items left in inventory do not add to the active Zoid's load.
Ordinary human supplies such as food, clothing, and small arms have negligible
weight on the Zoid Carry-capacity scale and are not charged against it.

You are overloaded only when equipped weight exceeds Carry capacity. At exactly
your capacity, you suffer no overload penalty.

When overloaded, the Zoid's base stats become 60% of catalog Speed, catalog
Reaction, and fixed and removable weapon base to-hit, rounding those reduced
values upward before later bonuses. This is not a blanket 40% reduction to
every final statistic.

TEMPORARY ARMOR AND DEFENSE
---------------------------
Temporary effects can raise or lower Armor and Defense, or modify other stats.
These adjustments normally persist until cleared by Resting, unless otherwise
stated.


==============================================================================
CHAPTER 3: EQUIPMENT AND LOADOUTS
==============================================================================

Your Zoid has up to four fixed weapon or system positions and four removable
equipment positions. A position may contain a weapon, a shield, a sensor, or
another utility system; an empty position grants no benefit.

FIXED AND REMOVABLE EQUIPMENT
-----------------------------
Fixed equipment is part of the selected Zoid form. Removable equipment is owned
as individual copies and assigned to compatible slots.

Assist slots accept compatible utility equipment. Weapon and Assist slots
accept both compatible weapons and utilities. Some Zoid-specific items have
additional mounting restrictions; their catalog entry takes precedence.

An item may be assigned to alternate forms of the same physical chassis. It
cannot be used simultaneously by two different owned Zoids. Owning two copies
allows the copies to be assigned separately.

Newly acquired Zoids and forms receive their named Zoid-specific removable
equipment.

PASSIVE AND ACTIVE SYSTEMS
--------------------------
A passive item contributes its bonuses whenever it is equipped. An active item
contributes its activated benefits only while enabled. Each item uses its own
state; activating one system does not activate another.

When an active piece of equipment is activated, you must pay its Energy Cost.
Active shields and invisibility equipment also pay upkeep at the Start of each
Turn, as described in Chapter 4.

Observe each item's listed duration. Expired or destroyed systems are
deactivated.

ACTIVATING EQUIPMENT IN COMBAT
------------------------------
Activating non-weapon equipment normally uses your standard action. A specific
ability may permit a bonus action instead, such as Fleet Footed, Shield Master,
or Smoke Master at the appropriate rank. You may only use a single bonus action
on a turn.

When you activate a speed-boosting unit as a standard action, you may also Dash
as part of that action. While the boost is active, you must use your entire
available movement, including the boost, each turn. Failing to do so ends the
boost. Activation by bonus action (as per the Fleet Footed ability) does not
itself grant the standard-action Dash benefit unless another rule says so.

At the start of a subsequent turn with speed-boosting equipment active, roll
d20 + PHY against 10 to withstand the G-force. On a failure, the boost ends at
the end of that turn.

You may voluntarily deactivate a speed boost as a free action.

Equipment that increases weapon Range or attack bonus may be activated using
your move action instead of your standard action. While it remains active,
moving deactivates it. Coasting in a flying Zoid does not count as movement for
this purpose.

WEAPON TAGS
-----------
Tags identify an attack's properties and which abilities apply to it. Examples
include Melee, Ranged, Laser, Particle, Explosive, Bullet, Piercing, and
Anti-Air.

Laser Expert requires the Laser tag. Rifle Expert requires both Ranged and
Bullet.

WEAPON PROPERTIES
-----------------
Ranged: Normal-range attacks have no range disadvantage. Close and Long attacks
normally have disadvantage. See Chapter 4 for the current band boundaries.

Close Range: May fire within 60m without range disadvantage and may be used
for attacks of opportunity. Its maximum range is half the Zoid's Range unless
the weapon explicitly states otherwise; it cannot fire at Long range.

Long Range: May fire at Long range, up to the Zoid's maximum Range, without
range disadvantage. It cannot fire within 60m.

Melee: May be used for attacks of opportunity and attacks within Reach:
30m for S, M, and L Zoids; 60m for LL; 90m for XL. This size-specific property
supplies the detail absent from the SRD's general 30m melee summary.

Laser, Explosive, Bullet, Particle: Respectively describe intense light/heat,
concussive force, physical projectiles, and high-energy particles. They identify
damage types for equipment and abilities; they do not alone grant extra damage.

Anti-Air: Removes the usual -10 penalty for attacking a flying Zoid. A flying
attacker does not gain this property benefit. Anti-Air Pro is a separate pilot
ability and still applies against flying targets regardless of weapon tags.

Piercing: Bypasses shields and damages the Zoid without damaging the shield.
Shield Master 4 overrides this property: Piercing-tagged weapons do not ignore
that pilot's shields. Piercing alone does not bypass Defense.

Acid/Defense: Attacks Reaction instead of Armor. On a hit, it deals no Health
damage; reduce Defense by the weapon's damage value and also reduce Armor by 3.
This is separate from the revised Armor Breaker ability, which uses its own
rank-based Armor reduction and does not add to the old Defense-reduction rule.

Homing: After a missed attack, you may reroll against the target's Reaction
instead of Armor. If that reroll hits, halve damage before applying defenses.
This is a reroll of the same attack, not permission to fire another weapon.
The SRD's phrase "before applying armor" is read as damage mitigation, since
Armor is a target number and Defense is the damage-reducing statistic.

Electrical: A hit can also cause System Freeze. A weapon with this tag has a
50% chance to apply this effect on a hit, and a 10% chance after a miss whose
attack total exceeds the target's Reaction. A miss that causes Freeze deals
no damage. An affected target may reroll at the end of its turn to shake off
the effect, using the original percentage.


==============================================================================
CHAPTER 4: COMBAT, ENERGY, AND REST
==============================================================================

Combat is a succession of decisions about position, targets, and resources.

INITIATIVE AND THE ROUND
------------------------
Use a 30m hex grid for ordinary Zoid combat. When combat begins, order the
participants by Reaction, highest first. Player pilots add Mental to Reaction,
as do certain NPCs at the GM's discretion.

Initiative ties should be resolved in favor of the players, in the case of
player vs. NPC, and at the discretion of the player or GM in the case of ally
vs. ally. In the case of player vs. player combat, resolve ties at the GM's
discretion.

A round represents six seconds. Each combatant takes one turn in initiative
order, then resolve the round's remaining environmental effects.

At the beginning of combat, set current Energy to half maximum, rounded down,
unless the GM states otherwise.

YOUR TURN AND REACTION
----------------------
On your turn you have one move action and one standard action. You need not
use either. You may split your movement around your standard action.

A standard action can attack, activate equipment, use a repair kit, or perform
another suitable task. Speech is a free action, subject to the six-second
round and the GM's limit on lengthy discussions. Other brief or simultaneous
activities may be free actions at the GM's discretion.

Some abilities permit specific bonus actions. Only one bonus action may be
taken per round.

You may use one reaction per round. Your reaction becomes available again at
the start of your next turn. Counter Attack and attacks of opportunity share
that allowance unless a specific rule says otherwise.

In very rare circumstances, you may be granted an additional action on your
turn. You may only take more than one action on your turn if a specific rule
states that you may.

AT THE START OF YOUR TURN
-------------------------
Follow these steps to track your Zoid's Energy Upkeep:

  1. Add EPT to current Energy, up to maximum Energy.
  2. Add the upkeep costs of your active shields and invisibility equipment.
  3. Subtract that total from the current Energy.

Each qualifying item costs its listed Energy Cost every turn it remains active.
An item that provides both shielding and invisibility is charged once, not
twice. Organoid-provided shields have no upkeep. Having an Organoid shield does
not make a separate equipment shield free. Smoke alone has no upkeep.

If Energy after recharge cannot pay the entire upkeep bill, all active
equipment is deactivated.

Example: Your Energy is 12 of 20 and your EPT is 4. An active shield costs 3
and active camouflage costs 5. Recharge raises Energy to 16; upkeep reduces
it to 8. If you instead began at 20, recharge would still stop at 20 and upkeep
would leave 12. Upkeep is paid after the cap is applied.

MAXIMUM ENERGY
--------------
For a player Zoid, maximum Energy equals:

  Catalog Energy + actual pilot level + active Organoid level
  + enabled equipment Energy bonuses.

EPT equals catalog recharge plus EPT upgrades, plus the pilot's Mental while
an Organoid is active. Type-ability bonus levels do not increase either the
actual-level Energy contribution or EPT.

A Zoid that is not in combat typically maintains an operating Energy level
equal to half of its maximum. Exceptions to this standard may be allowed at
the GM's discretion.

MAKING AN ATTACK
----------------
Choose a weapon and a target, determine the appropriate range or other
conditions, and check that the weapon is intact and affordable. Pay the listed
Energy Cost when you use it. A weapon attack rolls a d20; add the weapon's
to-hit modifier and any applicable condition modifiers. A total equal to or
greater than the target's Armor hits. If the attack explicitly targets
Reaction instead, use Reaction in place of Armor.

For an area-of-effect attack, every Zoid in the affected area may attempt to
dodge: roll d20 + Reaction. A result greater than or equal to the attacker's
modified attack roll avoids half of the damage.

Physical cover does not protect against an attack that hits all targets in
the area. Apply the weapon's additional targeting instructions where given.

After resolving damage, apply the weapon's additional effects and track any
recharge interval. Energy is spent even if the attack misses.

You may spend exactly your remaining Energy. Zero-cost equipment remains usable
at zero Energy.

MULTIPLE ATTACKS
----------------
Resolve each attack's targeting, Energy payment, and effects separately. A
Multi Attack ability does not waive the cost of the additional attacks. Its
rank-specific restrictions appear in Chapter 6.

Multiple Attacks per the Multi Attack abilities are all considered to be part
of the same action. If you lose your action on a turn, or use your action for
another purpose, the Multi Attack abilities do not give you an additional
action.

OTHER COMBAT ACTIONS
--------------------
Dash: Spend your standard action to gain an additional movement allowance up
to your Speed. A speed unit activated as a standard action can include this
Dash as described in Chapter 3.

Disengage: Spend your standard action to move out of enemy melee range without
provoking attacks of opportunity.

Aim: Spend your standard action to gain +10 to attacks on your next attack
action.

Dodge: Spend your standard action to gain +10 Armor and +10 Reaction until
the start of your next turn.

Feint: Spend your standard action to distract an enemy. Attacks against that
enemy by Zoids other than yours gain the Distracted modifier until the start
of your next turn.

Charge Energy: Spend your standard action to add your Zoid's EPT plus your
Mental to current Energy, capped at maximum. This is separate from the upkeep
at the Start of your Turn and does not pay upkeep on active equipment.

ATTACKS OF OPPORTUNITY
----------------------
Leaving an enemy's melee Reach provokes an attack of opportunity unless you
Disengage or another effect prevents it. The enemy must spend its reaction in
order to make an attack of opportunity. Only Melee and Close Range weapons can
make these attacks. Use the ordinary attack and Energy rules, including the
weapon's cost and target restrictions. The Mobile feat prevents these attacks
for the rest of a round in which you Dash.

RANGE BANDS
-----------
The Character Sheet uses these mutually exclusive bands for ranged attacks:

  Close:  Distance of 60m or less.
  Normal: More than 60m, up to and including half your current maximum Range.
  Long:   Beyond half your current maximum Range.

Close takes precedence. If your maximum Range is 100m, an attack at 55m is
Close, not Long. There is no Normal band for that maximum Range. If your
maximum Range is 500m, 250m is Normal and 251m is Long.

The Sniper ability's bonus applies only to Long-range ranged attacks.

Ordinary ranged attacks have disadvantage at Close and Long range, but not at
Normal range. Close Range and Long Range weapon properties modify these rules
as described in Chapter 3. A ranged attack cannot exceed the Zoid's maximum
Range in combat; a weapon may impose a shorter limit. At exactly maximum Range,
the attack is still in range, subject to its applicable range disadvantage.

Outside combat, the GM may allow a carefully lined-up long-distance shot or
a shot at an enormous target beyond the normal combat limit. This does not
extend combat Range automatically. Range previews do not enforce these target
limits or roll disadvantage for you.

DAMAGE
------
Current weapon profiles use flat damage. Apply the weapon's listed special
instructions, including any separate bursts or attacks.

For the Character Sheet's ordinary numerical damage preview:

  Damage = base damage
           + rounded(base damage x combined applicable damage percentage)
           + flat frame damage upgrade.

Organoid and Comeback percentages add together rather than multiplying one
another. Frame Melee Damage or Ranged Damage adds 20 damage per upgrade point,
and is applied after percentage multipliers.

Example: A weapon deals 100 base damage. A 20% Organoid damage bonus and 30%
Comeback bonus add 50 damage. Two matching damage-upgrade points add another
40, for 190 before critical hits and the target's defenses are resolved.

RESOLVING A NORMAL HIT
----------------------
If a shield intercepts the hit, apply damage to the shield as described in
Chapter 5. Otherwise subtract applicable Defense from the attack's damage
and deduct the result from Health. Defense cannot reduce the damage of a
normal hit below 1. Armor is not also subtracted; it was the target number
used to determine whether the attack hit.

Example: A normal hit deals 90 damage to an unshielded Zoid with 25 applicable
Defense. The target loses 65 Health. If applicable Defense were 100, it would
instead lose 1 Health. Attacks explicitly dealing no Health damage, such as
Armor Breaker, do not gain 1 damage from this minimum.

CRITICAL HITS
-------------
A natural 20 on the attack d20 is a critical hit. Ordinary critical hits
bypass Defense; they do not inherently double damage. A critical hit on a
shield deactivates that shield, and damage exceeding its remaining HP is
dealt to the Zoid's Health. See Chapter 5 for this shield-specific procedure.

Some abilities expand critical-hit range, change critical damage, or defend
against critical hits. Weapon Expert rank 2 allows a critical hit on a roll
of 19; ranks 3 and 4 crit on a roll of 18. At rank 4, critical hits with
qualifying weapons deal double damage. That doubling is not a bonus to every
ordinary hit.

Small Expert, certain size-Expert rank 4 abilities, Sniper 4, True Tank, and
Weapon Breaker add further critical-hit effects.

CRITICAL MISSES AND JAMS
------------------------
A natural 1 is a critical miss. A ranged weapon also jams. Clear the jam with
a standard action before firing it again, or immediately attempt d20 + MEN
against 15 to clear it without spending an action. The immediate Mental check
is available to a pilot, not an unpiloted wild Zoid.

A critical miss still costs the Energy paid for that use.

SYSTEM DOWN AND CORE DAMAGE
---------------------------
When a player Zoid, or a named NPC Zoid designated by the GM, reaches zero
Health, it suffers System Down. It is incapacitated, cannot move, and cannot
take actions. Its pilot checks d20 + PHY against 15 to remain conscious; an
unconscious pilot may retry that check each round.

On a conscious pilot's turn, attempt an emergency repair with d20 + MEN:

  Total 10 or less: The Core suffers 1 point of Core damage.
  Total 11-19:      The Zoid remains System Down, with no recovery.
  Total 20 or more: End System Down and restore the Zoid to 1 Health.

Each damaging attack received while System Down causes 1 Core damage. At
3 Core damage, the Core is permanently destroyed and must be replaced.
Lesser Core damage does not recover naturally; repair it at a Zoid Laboratory
by repairing all damage to the Zoid. Core damage is a separate tally, not
negative Health.

If the pilot loses consciousness but the Zoid can still act, use Pilot Down
in Chapter 12. That condition does not make a System Down chassis operational.

REST
----
Rest sets current Energy to half your equipped maximum, rounded down. It can
lower Energy that was above half as well as restore Energy that was below half.

Rest also clears the temporary Armor and Defense, or other stat adjustments.
It does not automatically heal Health, repair weapons, clear recorded shield
damage, or deactivate equipment.

The GM determines when a Rest is permitted and how long it takes.


==============================================================================
CHAPTER 5: SHIELDS, SMOKE, AND INVISIBILITY
==============================================================================

SHIELD POOLS
------------
Add the shield HP supplied by every active equipment shield and any active
White Organoid shield. If the combined pool is positive, add Shield Master's
bonus once. Multiple shield generators do not each receive a separate copy
of that ability's bonus.

Record shield damage separately from Health. Whenever a shield is activated
(even if deactivated and reactivated on the same turn), its Energy Cost is paid
and its health is set to its maximum. Follow the particular system's recharge
and duration text.

NORMAL HITS, CRITICAL HITS, AND PIERCING
----------------------------------------
A normal hit intercepted by a shield damages the shield instead of Health.
If it exhausts the shield, deactivate it. Any damage left over from that
ordinary hit is lost; it does not spill into Health.

A critical hit deactivates the shield even if damage is less than its remaining
HP. If the critical damage exceeds the shield's remaining HP, the excess
damages Health and normally bypasses Defense.

A Piercing-tagged attack bypasses shields without damaging them unless Shield
Master 4 prevents that bypass. Normal Defense still applies to a noncritical
Piercing hit to Health.

Example: A shield with 40 HP intercepts an ordinary 70-damage hit and fails;
the remaining 30 damage is lost. If the same hit is critical, the shield fails
and the 30 excess damages Health before any explicit critical-hit protection.

Example: Two active 200-HP shields and Shield Master 2 provide 550 total shield
HP: 200 + 200 + 150. After 120 shield damage, 430 remain. Both generators pay
their own upkeep at Start Turn. If instead the hit was a critical hit, after
the damage is calculated, both shields remain active. The critical hit
deactivates the one with the most HP remaining (200 + 30 from Shield Master),
leaving 200.

Shield Master 3 allows half your Defense to apply to damage done to shields.
Rank 4 retains that benefit and prevents Piercing-tagged weapons from ignoring
your shields. Resolve mitigation before recording the resulting shield damage.

SMOKE AND INVISIBILITY
----------------------
While protected by the relevant smoke or invisibility effect, a Zoid cannot
be targeted by attacks, but it can still be hit by area-of-effect attacks.
Observe the item's listed area, duration, and other restrictions.

The old Concealed attack modifiers do not override this newer prohibition on
targeting a Zoid protected by smoke or invisibility. Ordinary concealment from
terrain or other causes still uses the condition rules in Chapter 12.

Smoke Master modifies smoke coverage and duration, grants faster activation,
and at higher ranks extends its benefits to invisibility and allows
smoke/invisibility effects to persist briefly after leaving the
smoke/invisibility. It grants no protection from AoE attacks.

Invisibility equipment pays its Energy Cost each Start Turn while active.
Smoke equipment does not have an upkeep cost. Keep track of durations.


==============================================================================
CHAPTER 6: PILOT ABILITIES
==============================================================================

An ability is training you can bring to bear while piloting. Only active
abilities grant their active effects. A newly learned ability family begins
inactive. Learning a higher rank replaces the lower rank of that ability;
ranks are not separate bonuses that can be stacked in multiple slots.

You may have six active abilities, including no more than one size-Expert
ability. A displaced or deactivated ability remains owned and inactive.

Unless explicitly stated to be cumulative, use the current rank's text rather
than adding every numerical bonus from lower ranks.

TYPE ABILITIES
--------------
Ranks 1 through 5 grant +5, +10, +15, +20, and +25 effective pilot levels while
piloting a matching Zoid type. This affects style progression, not actual pilot
level or its Energy contribution. Use the strongest matching level bonus.

The catalog includes Tiger, Liger, Wolf, Insect, Gorilla, Raptor, T-Rex, Mammal,
Dinosaur, Flying, Reptile, and BLOX Type abilities. Eligibility follows the
listed type, not a broader biological interpretation. A Flying-type design is
not automatically treated as every type its animal shape might suggest.

SIZE EXPERTS
------------
These families grant 10%, 15%, 20%, and 25% at ranks 1 through 4 while piloting
an eligible size. The percentage contributes to Health, Armor, Reaction, Range,
and weapon to-hit. It does not increase every statistic on the sheet.

  Family                    Eligible size
  Small Expert              S
  Medium Expert             M
  Medium Under Expert       S or M
  Large Expert              L
  Large Large Expert        LL
  Large Large Over Expert   LL or XL
  Extra Large Expert        XL

Small Expert 2 also states: "Enemies of Size L or larger lose the benefits of
Critical Hits against you."

Small Expert 4, Medium Expert 4, and Medium Under Expert 4 also state:
"When making an attack against a target of size LL or XL, you subtract 2 from
your critical hit range."

Small Expert 3 and 4 retain rank 2's protection against critical hits from
enemies of Size L or larger.

WEAPON EXPERTS
--------------
Five families improve accuracy with qualifying weapons:

  Laser Expert      Weapons with the Laser tag.
  Missile Expert    Weapons with the Explosive tag.
  Particle Expert   Weapons with the Particle tag.
  Melee Expert      Weapons with the Melee tag.
  Rifle Expert      Weapons with both Ranged and Bullet tags.

  Rank    To-hit bonus     Additional critical-hit benefit
    1         +2          No additional critical rule in this rank.
    2         +3          Critical hits on a roll of 19.
    3         +4          Critical hits on a roll of 18.
    4         +5          Critical hits on a roll of 18; critical hits deal
                          double damage.

Only the strongest matching Weapon Expert to-hit bonus applies to a particular
weapon. Two matching Experts do not add their accuracy bonuses together.
Different weapons may benefit from different Experts. These rules apply to
fixed and removable weapons alike.

ANTI-AIR PRO
------------
All attacks against flying Zoids gain +2, +3, +4, or +5 to hit at ranks 1-4.
This is a benefit against the target, not a benefit restricted to weapons with
the Anti-Air tag. Apply it manually when the target qualifies.

FLEET FOOTED
------------
Gain +50, +100, +150, or +200 Speed at ranks 1-4.
At ranks 2, 3, and 4, speed-boosting equipment may be activated as a bonus
action.

HONED
-----
Gain +5, +10, +15, or +20 Reaction at ranks 1-4.

IRON DEFENSE
------------
Gain +10, +20, +30, or +40 Defense at ranks 1-4.

COMEBACK
--------
While current Health is at or below 20% of maximum Health, your attacks gain
20%, 30%, 40%, or 50% damage at ranks 1-4. The threshold includes exactly 20%.
This applies to melee and ranged attacks. See Chapter 4 for the calculation.

SNIPER
------
Gain +2, +4, +6, or +8 to hit at ranks 1-4 on qualifying long-range attacks.
The sheet applies this only to ranged attacks in the Long band.

Rank 4 also states: "Your critical range is reduced by 1."

TRUE TANK
---------
For every full 10 points of Defense gained from removable equipment,
gain 1 Armor. Total the eligible Defense before dividing by 10.

  Rank    Maximum Armor bonus    Additional benefit
    1             +5             None.
    2            +10             None.
    3            +10             Half your Defense is subtracted from damage
                                 you take from critical hits.
    4            +10             Your full Defense is subtracted from damage
                                 you take from critical hits.

The Armor cap stops increasing after rank 2.

Base Defense, Defense upgrades, Iron Defense, and temporary Defense adjustments
do not supply the removable-equipment Defense used to earn True Tank Armor.
The critical-hit benefit refers to your Defense, not only equipment Defense.

Example: Your removable equipment supplies 65 Defense. True Tank 1 grants 5
Armor because of its cap. True Tank 2, 3, or 4 grants 6 Armor. A separate
temporary +10 Defense does not increase that equipment-derived Armor bonus.

ARMOR BREAKER
-------------
When making an attack, you may make it against the target's Reaction. If it
hits, it deals no damage. Instead, reduce the target's Armor by your Armor
Breaker rank: 1, 2, 3, or 4. This is not additional normal damage and does not
reduce Defense.

EVASION MASTER
--------------
Attacks made against your Reaction take a -4, -8, -12, or -16 penalty to hit
you at ranks 1-4. This ability does not grant an Armor bonus or increase your
Reaction statistic. Apply the penalty to the incoming attack.

SHIELD MASTER
-------------
Increase the combined positive shield pool by the following amount:

  Rank    Shield HP bonus    Benefits gained at this rank
    1          +100         No additional benefit.
    2          +150         Shields can be activated as a bonus action.
    3          +200         Apply half Defense to damage done to shields.
    4          +250         Piercing-tagged weapons do not ignore your shields.

The additional benefits are cumulative. Rank 4 has all three additional
benefits, but its numerical bonus is +250, not the sum of ranks 1-4.
The ability does not create a shield when you have no active shield source.
The bonus Shield HP can be applied to one Shield at a time. Subsequent shields
activated simultaneously are not affected.

SMOKE MASTER
------------
Smoke units cover additional distance and gain the following cumulative effects:

  Rank    Extra coverage   Benefit gained at this rank
    1          15m         Smoke units last twice as long.
    2          15m         Smoke units can be activated as a bonus action.
    3          45m         Smoke Master applies to invisibility as well.
    4          45m         Smoke effects persist for 1 round after you leave
                           the smoke.

Each higher rank retains the earlier special benefits. Extra coverage is the
listed value for your rank, not the sum of all earlier values. Rank 4 does not
protect you from area-of-effect attacks.

ECONOMIZED SHOOTING
-------------------
Reduce the Energy Cost of Ranged-tagged weapons by 2, 4, 6, or 8 at ranks 1-4.
The reduced cost cannot fall below 2. A weapon whose original cost is already
below 2 keeps that cost; the ability does not make it more expensive.

Example: A cost-6 ranged weapon costs 4 with rank 1, and 2 with ranks 2-4.
An originally free weapon remains free.

MELEE MULTI ATTACK
------------------
Ranks 1-4 grant one, two, three, or four extra melee attacks per turn.

Only two melee attacks may be made against a single target with a single
action. Additional attacks from higher ranks must have a new target.

RANGED MULTI ATTACK
-------------------
Ranks 1-4 grant one, two, three, or four extra ranged attacks per turn.

Each ranged weapon can be fired only twice with one action. Additional ranged
attacks from higher ranks must be made with a different ranged weapon.

WEAPON BREAKER
--------------
Rank 1: When you score a critical hit with a melee weapon, one of the target's
weapons is destroyed at random.

Rank 2: When you score a critical hit with a melee weapon, choose one of the
target's weapons to destroy.

COUNTER ATTACK
--------------
Rank 1: When an enemy rolls a 1 on an attack against you, you may make a single
weapon attack against that enemy as a reaction.

Rank 2: When an enemy rolls a 5 or lower on an attack against you, you may make
a single weapon attack against that enemy as a reaction, whether or not its
attack hit.


==============================================================================
CHAPTER 7: FEATS
==============================================================================

Feats are distinctive talents that pilots pick up as they become highly skilled.
You gain a feat at level 10 and every ten levels thereafter.

PHALANX FIRE
------------
When attacking an enemy Zoid with a ranged weapon, you gain advantage on the
attack roll if an ally has dealt damage to that enemy Zoid this round.

SCRAPPY
-------
If no allied Zoids are within 60m of you and at least two enemy Zoids are within
60m of you, gain advantage on all attack rolls you make.

MOBILE
------
When you use your action to dash, you do not provoke attacks of opportunity for
the rest of the round.

OVERTUNED
---------
Your non-weapon Zoid equipment lasts twice as many rounds.

PIT STOP
--------
Spend only one action to perform a CAS swap during combat. You may reenter
battle on your next turn.

DEADSHOT
--------
If you do not move on your turn, gain +10 to ranged attacks.

DEMOLITIONIST
-------------
Once per Rest, you may completely fill your Zoid's Energy as a free action.
Record that use at the table and set current Energy to its maximum when you
invoke the feat.


==============================================================================
CHAPTER 8: ORGANOIDS
==============================================================================

An Organoid can transform the performance of its partner Zoid. The Character
Sheet supports White, Red, Blue, and Black Organoids at levels 0-40.

ORGANOIDS AND ORGANOID SYSTEMS
------------------------------
Zoids possess a measure of sentience, and wild Zoids can act without pilots.
A true Organoid is an exceptionally rare intelligent partner. An Organoid
System, or OS, is technology designed to reproduce its functions.

  Limited OS: Grants no statistical bonuses, but allows autonomous operation
  if the pilot is unconscious. No numerical Organoid level is specified. Do
  not assign statistical bonuses to a Limited OS merely because it enables
  autonomous behavior.

  Normal OS: Its level equals half the pilot's actual level, rounded down.
  Its color is set when the Zoid is constructed.

  True Organoid: Its level equals the pilot's actual level, to a maximum of 40.
  Its color follows the pilot's personality, as judged by the GM.

  Wild OS: Reverse-engineered from a deceased Organoid. It functions like a
  true Organoid and follows personality-based color, but always remains active.
  If it is a white color, its shield is activated when initiative order is
  determined.

Choose the color and level, then activate the Organoid as an action to gain
its benefits. Selecting those settings without activation grants no bonuses.
Use the appropriate color-and-level table for its Health, Armor, Speed, Range,
attack, and damage contributions.

Only an active White Organoid provides Organoid shield HP. Red, Blue, and Black
Organoids do not create shield HP. White shield strength uses Organoid level,
not pilot level.

ACTIVATION AND RESOURCES
------------------------
An Organoid or OS normally requires a standard action to activate. Wild OS
uses its special rule above. It remains active only while a pilot occupies
the cockpit, and deactivates when the pilot leaves or the Zoid is destroyed
or suffers System Down. An unconscious pilot is still in the cockpit; see
Pilot Down for autonomous operation.

Activating an Organoid adds the increase in maximum Health to current Health
and the increase in maximum Energy to current Energy. Existing damage and
spent-Energy deficits are therefore preserved.

While active, add Organoid level to maximum Energy and pilot Mental to EPT.
Deactivation removes its bonuses; current Energy is clamped to the reduced
maximum where necessary.

Organoid shields pay no per-turn Energy upkeep. Other active shields and
invisibility equipment continue to pay their own costs.


==============================================================================
CHAPTER 9: UPGRADES AND REPAIRS
==============================================================================

FRAME UPGRADES
--------------
Upgrades belong to a physical chassis and follow its acquired CAU forms.
Each track has its own rank and price progression.

  Track           Benefit per point                Maximum points
  Speed           +30 Speed                        5
  Reaction        +1 Reaction                      5
  Defense         +5 Defense                       No cap
  Capacity        +10 Carry capacity               5
  Accuracy        +1 to final attack accuracy       No cap
  Armor           +1 Armor                         5
  EPT             +1 Energy Per Turn               No cap
  Melee Damage    +20 matching weapon damage        5
  Ranged Damage   +20 matching weapon damage        5

Accuracy applies to fixed and removable attacks. Armor is a flat addition
after percentage bonuses. Damage tracks apply to matching fixed, generic
removable, and Zoid-specific removable weapons, after damage percentages.
Melee-tagged weapons use Melee Damage; Ranged weapons without Melee use Ranged
Damage. Non-damaging utilities gain no damage bonus.

PRICE OF THE NEXT POINT
-----------------------
For tracks other than Armor and EPT, buying rank N costs 5,000g x N.
Armor rank N costs (5,000g x N) + 20,000g.
EPT rank N costs 10,000g x 2^(N-1).

  Rank      Ordinary track     Armor         EPT
    1           5,000g         25,000g       10,000g
    2          10,000g         30,000g       20,000g
    3          15,000g         35,000g       40,000g
    4          20,000g         40,000g       80,000g
    5          25,000g         45,000g      160,000g

These are the prices of individual points, not the total price of reaching a
rank. EPT upgrades do not refill Energy or change the half-maximum Rest rule.

REPAIRS
-------
Health repair costs 10g per point actually restored, up to current maximum
Health. You may perform a limited repair or repair all missing Health.

Repairing a broken weapon costs 10,000g. An intact weapon requires no payment.

Health repair does not repair weapons. Weapon repair does not repair Health.
Repairs by hand require a Zoid Institute or comparable facilities, sufficient
time, and repair parts worth the stated 10g per Health restored. Unless
specified by the GM, repairs can be completed over the course of a Rest.

CONSUMABLE REPAIR KITS
----------------------
CA Ion repair kits are rare consumables based on ancient technology. Using
one in combat takes a bonus action. The listed sizes restore:

  S: 50 Health     M: 150 Health     L: 300 Health     Z: 50% HP

Follow a kit's current equipment entry where it provides more detail. Do not
exceed maximum Health.

Core damage uses the Laboratory and full-repair requirement in Chapter 4;
ordinary Rest does not erase it.


==============================================================================
CHAPTER 10: THE HANGAR
==============================================================================

KNOWN DESIGNS AND CONSTRUCTION
------------------------------
After your free starter, a normal Zoid purchase requires the design's Zi Data,
its listed gold price, the required Core and Parts, and any required owned
prerequisite Zoid or form.

The recipe specifies what is consumed. Where several owned copies qualify,
choose the particular chassis to use. A chassis that still owns CAUs cannot
be consumed as an ordinary construction component. Ordinary replacement
builds do not inherit their consumed chassis's upgrades.

Keep gold, known designs, and material quantities accurate. Removable equipment
remains owned when its host is consumed or sold.

THE COMPONENTS OF DEVELOPMENT
-----------------------------
Zi Data contains the design's blueprints, operating system, control code, and
the interface between its Core and body. Knowing a design is distinct from
owning the materials needed to build it.

A Zoid Core contains the creature's biomechanical organs. An intact recovered
Core can be reused in a compatible new body requiring that Core type. If a
development recipe replaces a base Zoid's Core with a different type, its old
Core can be retained or sold. Record such recovery manually; it does not waive
the current recipe's required inputs or change its purchase price.

Rare Parts must be found, bought, or salvaged. Standardized generic parts are
represented by the recipe's gold price; you need not list every manufactured
component.

PAINT AND PERSONALIZATION
-------------------------
A basic custom-color paint job costs 500g for an S Zoid, 1,000g for M or L,
5,000g for LL, and 10,000g for XL. This is an optional expense and provides no
statistical benefit outside of roleplaying.

SALVAGE
-------
When you destroy an enemy Zoid outside an organized Battle, choose what you
are trying to preserve: its Core, Zi Data, Rare Parts, or Supplies. Harvest
the selected category rather than claiming all four. The choice is made when
the Zoid is destroyed, reflecting the care needed to preserve useful salvage.

Supplies are generic parts worth half the destroyed Zoid's Development Cost.
They may be sold or credited toward a new build. A usable Core can supply a
compatible recipe, and Rare Parts can supply their listed construction needs.
The GM must determine recoverability and record the salvage or credit.

When salvaging Zi Data, you may take the data for one of the destroyed Zoid's
construction-chain base designs instead of its own design. This grants the
chosen design, not every design in the chain.

Example: You already know Proto Zaber's design but lack Zaber Fang Old, one
of its construction ancestors. You may choose that ancestor's Zi Data rather
than Proto Zaber's data or another salvage category. These salvage rules are
distinct from the 75% sale refund for an owned Zoid.

CAU FORMS
---------
An acquired CAU is an alternate form belonging to its host chassis. Each form
has its own loadout and activation states, while chassis upgrades and current
resources remain associated with the physical Zoid.

Forms share current Health. Changing to a form with a lower Health maximum
clamps the pool; changing back does not refill it. Switching forms is not a
repair or a way to erase damage.

Some forms supersede earlier forms. For example, Shield Liger DCS-J replaces
DCS, and Zaber Fang TS replaces AT. Use the current recipe for prerequisites.

Detaching a CAU refunds half its current listed price and returns its applicable
Parts. Host upgrades remain; form-specific records are removed. Removable
equipment stays owned.

CHANGING ARMOR IN COMBAT
------------------------
CAS means Changing Armor System; a CAU is a Changing Armor Unit. Move to a CAU
station and spend a standard action to begin the swap. Spend one standard
action on each of two subsequent turns to complete it. Once begun, the swap
cannot be aborted. On the completion turn, you may use movement to return to
the battle.

Pit Stop requires you to spend only one action to perform the swap, and you
may reenter battle on your next turn.

SELLING A ZOID
--------------
Selling an owned chassis and its acquired CAUs returns 75% of their combined
current listed prices, rounded down if necessary. It removes that chassis,
its forms, and its upgrades. Removable equipment remains in your inventory.
Parts and upgrades do not receive an additional sale refund.

FUZOR CONSTRUCTION
------------------
FUZOR recipes require the known design and distinct owned component Zoids.
Their listed fusion gold cost is 0g. Components with acquired CAUs cannot be
consumed. A newly assembled FUZOR is an independent chassis; it does not add
its components' upgrades to its own statistics.

  Fuzor Dragon:   Leo Blaze, Unenlagia, Mosasledge, Nightwise.
  Chimera Dragon: Diplo Gun, Shell Karn, Demon's Head, Flyscissors.
  Gojulox:        Diplo Gun, Shell Karn, Demon's Head, Leo Blaze,
                 Unenlagia, Nightwise.
  2-Arm Lizard:   Leo Blaze, Unenlagia.
  Griffin:       Leo Blaze, Unenlagia, Buster Eagle.
  Lord Gale:     Diplo Gun, Shell Karn, Demon's Head, Flyscissors.

As an action in combat, a pilot may take the Fuze action. If allied Zoids within
60m take the Fuze action within the next round, the Zoids may Fuze if they meet
the requirements listed above.

TRANSPORTS
----------
Each loaded owned chassis occupies one bay; its acquired CAUs travel with it.
Use its selected form to check the maximum permitted size. An allied Zoid also
occupies one bay.

  Transport       Bays       Largest permitted Zoid
  Gustav            2        M
  Hover Cargo       6        L
  Dragoon Nest     12        LL
  Whale King       24        XL

You cannot exceed either capacity or size restrictions. Unload a Zoid before
changing to a form that its current Transport cannot carry.


==============================================================================
CHAPTER 11: MOVEMENT, TRAVEL, AND TERRAIN
==============================================================================

COMBAT MOVEMENT
---------------
The standard battle map uses 30m hexes. On your turn, your move action allows
you to travel up to your Speed in meters. You may move, take a standard action,
and then spend the rest of your movement. Dash adds another movement allowance
up to Speed. A round lasts six seconds; do not multiply Speed by six to find
your movement allowance.

For grid movement, round distances to the nearest increment of the grid size,
normally 30m.

NARRATIVE TRAVEL
----------------
Outside turn-by-turn situations, read Speed as kilometers per hour. Use this
for long-distance travel and exploration without immediate hazards. The GM
accounts for routes, obstacles, and circumstances rather than resolving every
six-second round. Flying or swimming routes may shorten travel by bypassing
obstacles and taking a more direct path.

JUMPING
-------
A land-based Zoid can make a running jump up to one-third of its maximum Speed,
rounded to the nearest 30m, if it has room to run the same distance before
jumping. From a standstill, it can jump only 30m. Jumping uses the move action;
it is not a separate free movement allowance. Temporary Speed boosts increase
running-jump distance.

FLYING, SWIMMING, AND COASTING
------------------------------
A Zoid cannot fly or swim unless it is specifically designed to do so.
An aerial or marine design moves through its intended medium at its Speed.

A flying Zoid must move at least 60m each turn or stall and fall. This mandatory
movement is Coasting. If it moves only those 60m and no more, treat it as not
having moved for other rules, including equipment that requires stationary
operation and attacks of opportunity. Coasting still changes its position.

Restrained flying Zoids fall 180m each turn and take 50 damage per 180m fallen,
ignoring Armor.

DIFFICULT TERRAIN
-----------------
Ice, bogs, loose sand, shallow water, or similar terrain may hinder a Zoid not
designed for it. If the GM determines the terrain is hazardous for that design,
halve its Speed while moving on that terrain.

STEALTH
-------
Without special equipment, hiding a multi-ton machine is usually a matter of
route, cover, and sensor exposure rather than a quiet step. Use terrain to
avoid detection, or equip technology designed to conceal the Zoid and dampen
its sound. The GM determines detection based on the situation and equipment.


==============================================================================
CHAPTER 12: CONDITIONS
==============================================================================

Conditions describe changes to what a Zoid can do and how attacks involving
it are resolved. Apply their modifiers manually. If both attacker and defender
have a condition, apply both relevant modifiers unless a specific rule prevents
the attack altogether.

SYSTEM DOWN
  The Zoid is incapacitated and cannot move or take actions. Use the emergency
  repairs and Core damage rules in Chapter 4. Incoming melee attacks
  automatically score critical hits; incoming ranged attacks gain +10 to hit.

SYSTEM FREEZE
  The Zoid is stunned and cannot move until the condition ends. Incoming melee
  attacks automatically score critical hits; incoming ranged attacks gain
  +10 to hit. Use the causing effect's duration or recovery rule.

PILOT DOWN
  The pilot is unconscious. A Zoid capable of continuing autonomously acts
  without the pilot's active abilities. For this condition, treat pilot level
  as 1, or as the Organoid System's level if it has one. This is a temporary
  combat calculation, not permanent loss of levels or learned abilities.

RESTRAINED
  The Zoid cannot use movement. Its ranged attacks take -5 to hit; ranged
  attacks against it gain +5. A restrained flying Zoid falls as described in
  Chapter 11.

BLINDED
  Visual optics cannot see. Radar or another system may still detect objects.
  The Zoid's attacks take -20 to hit; attacks against it gain +10.

SLOWED
  The Zoid's attacks take -5 to hit; attacks against it gain +5. Its Speed is
  also reduced by half unless another amount is specified.

HIGH GROUND
  A Zoid standing on solid ground at least 100m above another Zoid has High
  Ground relative to it. Its ranged attacks gain +10 to hit, and ranged
  attacks against it take -5. Merely flying overhead is not High Ground.

CONCEALED
  Apply this to an exchange where at least one Zoid is not visible to the
  other. Radar detection is not, by itself, visual sight. The GM judges
  visibility and equipment interactions. A Concealed attacker gains +10 to
  hit; attacks against a Concealed defender take -20. Blinded can also apply.
  The newer smoke/invisibility rule takes precedence: a protected Zoid cannot
  be targeted by attacks, though AoE may still hit it. Do not use a Concealed
  penalty to turn that prohibited targeted attack into an allowed one.

COVERED
  A physical obstacle obscures at least half of the Zoid from the attacker's
  sight, as determined by the GM. Another Zoid can provide such an obstacle.
  Ranged attacks against the Covered Zoid have disadvantage. Attacks affecting
  all targets in an area ignore this cover.

FLYING
  The Zoid gains +10 to its attacks. Attacks against it take -10 unless the
  attack benefits from the Anti-Air property. A flying attacker cannot benefit
  from that property.

AIMING
  Gain +10 to attacks on your next attack action after taking Aim.

DODGING
  Gain +10 Armor and +10 Reaction until the start of your next turn after
  taking Dodge.

DISTRACTED
  Attacks against the Zoid gain +10 to hit. When Feint causes the condition,
  it benefits attackers other than the feinting Zoid until the start of the
  feinting Zoid's next turn.

APPENDIX A: USING THE COMPANION APPS
==============================================================================

THE CHARACTER SHEET
-------------------
Use Overview for pilot details, Organoids, and your owned-Zoid hangar. Combat
records resources and weapon use. Upgrades manages frame improvements and
equipment copies. Pilot holds abilities, feats, notes, materials, and known
designs. Transport manages your carrier and its cargo.

The sheet calculates supported modifiers, attack previews, Energy payments,
upkeep, and purchases. It does not select targets or decide hits, criticals,
status effects, damage mitigation, or most action-economy permissions for you.

Normal OS mode calculates half the actual pilot level, rounded down, within
the supported level range of 0-40. Manual Organoid levels remain available.
The Combat page shows the AoE half-damage rule; resolve that roll at the table.

The supplementary SRD procedures in this handbook are tabletop rules, not new
automations. In particular, milestone awards, rank prerequisites, rest-based
ability swaps, initiative, Energy resets at combat start, advantage, jams,
conditions, G-force checks, Charge Energy, Core damage, and salvage are manual.

Use the displayed modifier with the recorded raw d20. Enter resulting Health
and shield damage after resolving incoming attacks. Save the character to keep
your changes; recalculating or previewing an attack does not spend resources.

THE ENCOUNTER TRACKER
---------------------
Each enemy has independent editable statistics, Energy, EPT, and equipment
costs. Its calculator applies supported catalog and ability bonuses; the GM
may edit the results for the encounter.

For equipment, mark Active, enter Shield HP where applicable, and mark Grants
invisibility where applicable. Known systems are populated from the catalog.
The tracker combines active shield HP, adds Shield Master once, and subtracts
recorded shield damage. Organoid shield HP is a separate upkeep-free input.

Start Turn recharges, then pays upkeep using the displayed costs. Shield damage,
custom capacities, activation states, and invisibility flags are saved with
the enemy set. Older saved sets begin with equipment inactive when that state
was not previously recorded.

Enemy sets intentionally preserve saved numerical edits. Refresh an enemy's
catalog loadout deliberately when you want newly calculated values. The tracker
does not reproduce all player-sheet systems: contextual Sniper bonuses, full
Organoid stat progression, equipment-derived True Tank Armor, critical effects,
and targeting rules still require GM handling. It also retains its own enemy
required-level data for encounter construction.


==============================================================================
APPENDIX B: RULES REQUIRING A TABLE RULING
==============================================================================

MULTIPLE SHIELDS
  Multiple activated shields should be numbered in the order they are
  activated. Damage that overwhelms a shield carries over the shield beneath
  it. Critical hits deactivate any shield they deal damage to.
  Shield Master is applied only to the first shield activated.

MITIGATION AND RESTORATION
  Changes to stats in combat last until a Rest, unless otherwise specified.

MOVEMENT EDGE CASES
  Movement on a 30m cell grid rounds down to the nearest multiple of 30m.
  Flying Zoids cannot normally stall unless Restrained.

DURATION AND ACTIVATION
  Overtuned and Smoke Master or Shield Master add additional increments of
  duration, rather than multiplying twice. A unit that lasts 3 turns would
  last 9 turns, when both apply, not 12 turns.

  The range increase from Smoke Master does not apply to units that grant
  invisibility.

  A unit that would deactivate due to its duration deactivates at the start
  of a turn, before upkeep is calculated.

ADVANCEMENT AND DOWNTIME
  Exact Rest duration is determined by the GM.
  Leveling up, as well as Zoid Upgrades or Development, must take place during
  a Rest.

PILOTS ON FOOT
  For interaction that cannot be handled narratively, use a d20 roll versus
  either 10 or 15, adding Pilot Attributes as appropriate.

APPENDIX C: QUICK REFERENCE
==============================================================================

ROUND AND TURN
  Six-second rounds; initiative by Reaction, highest first. One move action
  and one standard action per turn. One reaction, refreshed at your next turn.
  Abilities grant specific bonus-action permissions, limited to one per round.
  Reset Energy to half at combat start unless the GM says otherwise.

ATTACKS
  d20 + modifiers >= Armor hits; substitute Reaction where instructed.
  An AoE avoidance roll equal to or above the attack total avoids half damage.
  Advantage: higher of two d20s. Disadvantage: lower of two d20s.
  Normal unshielded damage: subtract applicable Defense, minimum 1.
  Natural 20: critical; normally bypasses Defense and deactivates a shield,
  with critical excess damage reaching Health. Natural 1: critical miss;
  a ranged weapon jams.

START TURN (UPKEEP)
  Recharge Energy by EPT, up to maximum, then subtract all active
  shield/invisibility upkeep.
  Organoid shields are free. If upkeep is unaffordable, deactivate all active
  equipment.

USE
  Pay listed Energy. Numerical-damage weapons roll a d20 and add modifiers to
  hit. Utilities do not.
  A broken weapon or unaffordable cost prevents the use.

REST
  Set Energy to half maximum, rounded down. Reset temporary Armor and Defense,
  or other temporarily modified stats.
  Do not automatically heal or repair weapons.

ABILITIES
  Six active slots. One active Size Expert. Higher ranks replace lower ranks.

  Weapon Expert to-hit                  +2        +3        +4        +5

  Shield Master HP                    +100      +150      +200      +250
  True Tank Armor cap                   +5       +10       +10       +10
  Evasion Master incoming penalty       -4        -8       -12       -16
  Economized Shooting reduction          2         4         6         8
  Smoke Master extra coverage          15m       15m       45m       45m

ECONOMIZED SHOOTING
  Minimum reduced cost 2. Originally lower costs are unchanged.

REPAIRS
  Health: 10g per point restored. Broken weapon: 10,000g.

MULTIPLE ZOIDS
  Each physical Zoid keeps its own resources. Forms share their chassis's pool.
